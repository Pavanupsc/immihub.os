Documents Every H-1B Holder Should Know

Five documents define your status in the U.S. — keep them current, organized, and easy to find.

Passport

Government-issued travel document. Most U.S. visa stamps require your passport to remain valid for at least 6 months beyond your stay.

I-797 Approval Notice

USCIS notice confirming your H-1B petition was approved. Defines your authorized employment start and end dates.

I-94 Travel Record

Proof of your lawful entry and the date by which you must depart or extend status. Issued electronically by CBP.

U.S. Visa Stamp

The visa foil placed in your passport at a U.S. consulate. Required for re-entry into the U.S. after international travel.

EAD Card

Employment Authorization Document. Issued in specific scenarios — H-4 EAD, OPT, GC-pending — and tied to a strict expiry date.

A Year in the Life of Your Status

A typical compliance year for an H-1B holder. Use it as a mental model — your specific timeline depends on your case.

180+ days out

Long-range planning

Confirm passport validity, scan all documents into your vault, and map upcoming deadlines for the year.

90 days out

H-1B extension window opens

USCIS accepts H-1B extension petitions up to 6 months before your I-797 expires — earlier is calmer.

60 days out

Travel & visa stamping

If you'll need a fresh visa stamp, line up consulate appointments and confirm passport pages.

30 days out

Document refresh

Re-check I-94 against the latest entry, confirm employer records, and tidy your vault for any pending file.

7 days out

Final compliance check

Final reminder window. By now, every dependent document should be filed, scanned, and confirmed.

Glossary of Common Terms

Plain-English definitions for the acronyms that show up most often.

I-797

USCIS notice of action — most often confirming approval of your H-1B petition and listing your authorized validity period.

I-94

Arrival/departure record issued by CBP, showing your authorized period of stay in the U.S.

EAD

Employment Authorization Document — a card permitting work in specific status categories.

USCIS

U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services — the agency that adjudicates immigration benefits.

CBP

U.S. Customs and Border Protection — issues your I-94 at the port of entry.
